How experienced operators estimate

No single shortcut owns the whole decision. Fast operators screen with broad rates, isolate the expensive systems, then replace guesses with a scope of work.

FASTEST · LEAD SCREEN

Price per square foot

Multiply subject living area by a local rehab rate: light $15–25/sf, medium $25–45/sf, heavy $45–75/sf in the cited 2026 wholesaler guide.

Best for
Under-a-minute triage.
Strength
Consistent and fast.
Weakness
Wet rooms and failed systems can overwhelm the average.
FIRST 5 MINUTES

“Big 5” system check

Price roof, HVAC, plumbing, electrical, and foundation before finishes. The label is generic industry practice, not credited to one educator.

Best for
Fast go / no-go walkthrough.

FINAL-OFFER DETAIL

Room-by-room line items

Walk every room and price floors, walls, ceilings, cabinets, counters, fixtures, and windows. Kitchens and baths alone can require 15–20 line items.

Best for
A property you may actually contract.
Strength

Weakness

SYSTEMATIC FRAMEWORK

J Scott’s scope of work

Define the SOW, break it into 25 renovation components, and price 150+ common tasks using a local price book. Refresh inputs as labor and materials move.

Best for
Repeatable investor underwriting.
Strength
Auditable and teachable.
Weakness
Upfront work; book figures are not current local prices.

≤ 10%gross living area variance; another cited guide uses ±200 sf
Matchbeds, baths, style, build era, parking, lot, and target condition
Weightthe closest, most recent, most comparable evidence higher

Where beginners lose the deal

  • Cherry-picking the highest sale instead of the best comp.
  • Using $/sq ft as the offer basis instead of a screening estimate.
  • Comparing a planned renovation to dated or fixer-upper sales.
  • Skipping a 10–20% repair contingency or hiding it inside hoped-for profit.
  • Trusting an automated valuation without driving the comps and checking the block.
  • Forgetting that the end buyer still needs a real margin after financing, holding, closing, and resale costs.
